Tables

A Duck Creek table stores its values as comma-separated rows under <data>, with the row and column keys listed separately. A line merge sees one long row of numbers and conflicts whenever two people touch the same row. The driver instead reads every cell by its keys, so two changes to different cells of one row merge cleanly, and only a cell both sides changed differently is a conflict.

Which tables merge by cell

Section titled “Which tables merge by cell”

The driver merges a <table> cell by cell when it has a separator attribute and holds exactly one <fields>, exactly one <data>, at least one <rowKeys> and at least one <colKeys>, and nothing else, comments included. Tables with several row or column axes qualify too.

Each cell is matched by its row keys, its column keys and its field name, never by its position in the row. A column one side inserted does not shift the other side’s values.

If the table’s shape changed in a way the driver cannot line up, such as keys reordered on one side, it leaves the whole table as one conflict. Any other table merges like ordinary elements.

Open a table

Section titled “Open a table”

In the Structural diff, a table shows as one row with a count such as ✕ 2 conflicts · 5 changes and a ⊞ review button. Press ⊞ review to open the table in its own tab, next to the case’s tab.

In a table tab the ribbon’s tabs are Session, Resolve and View. Under the tab bar:

  • The table’s name, with 3 auto-merged for cells the driver settled, 2 to review for conflicts, and silent drop if the driver lost any cell.
  • A line such as 2 conflicts remaining across all pages, and where the current conflict is.
  • If the table is split into pages, one tab per page, each with its count of open conflicts. A table is split when one of its row axes is marked as the page axis (pageKey="1").

Settle cells in the grid

Section titled “Settle cells in the grid”

The grid shows the table with its keys down the left and across the top.

  • A cell in conflict is split in two: ← ours on top, → theirs below, and the base value under both. Click the value you want.
  • A cell one side changed shows the new value, with the old one struck out.
  • ⊖ marks a removed value and ▲ a silent drop.

From the keyboard, Tab to a conflict cell. O and T take ours or theirs and move on to the next conflict, ← and → move between conflicts without taking, and U reopens the last pick. ↶ undo pick does the same. Prev, Next, Take ours and Take theirs on the Resolve tab walk the cells across every page.

A row is written into the merged result only when every conflicting cell in it has a pick. Cells the driver merged keep their merged values, so picking ours for one cell never undoes a change theirs made to another cell in that row.

The list view

Section titled “The list view”

List on the View tab shows one line per conflicting cell instead of the grid.

A small map at the top marks where the conflicts and changes sit. Each line names the cell by its keys and field, shows base, ours and theirs, and has take ours and take theirs. The folded sections at the bottom list the cells and rows the driver merged on its own. Grid switches back, and Only conflicts dims every cell that is not in conflict.

Rows and tables that need a whole side

Section titled “Rows and tables that need a whole side”

Some changes cannot be settled one cell at a time. The grid says so and offers a whole side instead:

  • A row one side deleted and the other edited. Keep the editing side’s row with its edits, or drop it as the deleting side did.
  • ⇅ order shift. The row or column order changed on one side. Taking a side keeps that side’s whole table and discards the other side’s other edits to it.
  • Cell counts that do not line up. A row has a different number of values than its keys call for. Take one side’s whole row.

Bulk picks

Section titled “Bulk picks”

All ours and All theirs cover the whole file

All ours and All theirs on the Resolve tab settle every open conflict in the merged result, not just this table’s, and they take whole rows: in a row with a conflict, they also drop the other side’s changes to that row’s other cells. Undo reverses them in one step. To keep both sides’ changes in a row, pick cell by cell.

The same holds for a table conflict settled from the case’s own tab with Take ours or Take theirs: there the conflict is the whole <row>. Settle tables in the table tab.

Finish

Section titled “Finish”

Picks in the table tab go straight into the case’s merged result. When the table is done, the line above the grid says all its conflicts are resolved. Go back to the case’s tab to finish the rest of the file and save it; see Saving and finishing.
