Resolving conflicts

A conflict is a place where ours and theirs both changed the same element in different ways. The driver writes both versions into the merged result between conflict markers and leaves the choice to you.

Step to a conflict

Section titled “Step to a conflict”

The ribbon’s take buttons act on the conflict you have stepped to, and when a case opens, no conflict is picked yet: the CONFLICT readout on the Merge tab shows 0 / and the total. Press Next or Prev to step to one. The readout then shows which one you are on, for example 2 / 5, and the panes scroll to it. Stepping past the last conflict wraps around to the first.

Right-clicking a line inside a conflict, or using the Structural diff, works on that conflict without stepping to it first.

Settle a conflict

Section titled “Settle a conflict”

There are five ways to settle a conflict. They all change the same merged text, so use whichever is closest to hand, and mix them freely.

From the ribbon

Section titled “From the ribbon”

With a conflict stepped to, use the RESOLVE group on the Merge tab.

Button What it does Key
Take ours Keeps ours and drops theirs. O
Take theirs Keeps theirs and drops ours. T
Take both Keeps both, ours first. Its menu also has Theirs first. B, Shift+B
Isolate Splits a conflict that covers several elements into one conflict per element.
Undo Reverses the last change to the merged result. U

The letter keys work anywhere except the merged pane, where they type as in any editor. ← and → do the same as Prev and Next while the focus is outside the panes, for example after you click a ribbon button.

Isolate is for a conflict the driver wrote as one block, such as a run of unnamed elements that both sides changed. It pairs the elements by position and gives each pair its own conflict, so you can take ours for one and theirs for the next.

Undo works on the merged result as a whole, so it also reverses hand edits. Inside the merged pane, Ctrl+Z does the same.

From a pane

Section titled “From a pane”

Right-click any line in any pane, or press Shift+F10, for a menu that acts on that line.

Group Items
Resolve Take ours, Take theirs, Take both: Ours first, Take both: Theirs first, Isolate, Inspect element, Undo
Clipboard Cut, Copy, Paste, Select all. Cut and Paste appear in the merged pane only.
Editor Find, Replace (merged pane only), Go to line, Toggle fold

The take items act on the conflict on that line. When they are grayed out, a note under the heading says why, for example No conflict on this line.

From the Structural diff

Section titled “From the Structural diff”

Click a conflict row’s chip, or right-click the row, for its menu: Take ours, Take theirs or Keep both. Keep both is the same as Take both on the ribbon: ours first, then theirs.

Once a conflict is settled, however you settled it, its chip changes to resolved.

From the line strip

Section titled “From the line strip”

Click a line inside a conflict in ours, base or theirs. The line strip compares that line across the three inputs, and in the box marked both, the ours and theirs values are buttons. Click one to take that side for the whole conflict.

Edit the merged result

Section titled “Edit the merged result”

The merged pane is an ordinary editor. Type the value you want, delete a side, or merge the two sides by hand, then delete the marker lines. The conflict counts as settled once its markers are gone.

A line counts as a conflict marker the way git counts one: seven <, =, > or | characters at the start of the line, followed by a space or the end of the line. While you work, the Workbench numbers each conflict’s first marker line, for example <<<<<<< ours #1.

Read the Structural diff

Section titled “Read the Structural diff”

The Structural diff on the left lists the elements that changed and what the driver did with each one. Read down it before you resolve anything: it is where you spot the driver keeping a change you did not expect. Click a row to show the element in every pane.

Each row names the element, like <private id="deductible">, and ends with a chip for the driver’s verdict.

Chip Meaning
merged Both sides changed the element, and the driver combined the changes.
took ours, took theirs Only one side changed it, so the driver kept that side.
reordered One side moved the element among its siblings.
added, removed A table row that was added or removed, merged without a conflict.
conflict Both sides changed it in different ways. You decide.
silent drop The driver’s output is missing something one side had.
resolved A conflict you have settled.

Small badges under the name say which side changed what: local △ and remote △ for a change on ours or theirs, local +attr for an attribute ours added, and order changed.

Silent drops

A silent drop means the driver lost data. Compare the merged result with ours and theirs, put the missing part back by hand, and report it with Report driver defect on the row’s menu.

Showing more or less

Section titled “Showing more or less”
  • Changed, the default, lists only elements that changed. All shows the whole document as a tree, with runs of unchanged elements folded into bars you can open.
  • danger only narrows Changed to conflicts and silent drops.
  • The line under the header counts changed elements, open conflicts and unresolved fallbacks, and the path under that shows where the selected element sits.
  • ‹ folds the panel to a narrow rail, with a count of open conflicts and drops. Structure on the Merge tab brings it back.

Click a row, then ↑ and ↓ move between rows, and Enter opens the row’s menu.

Look closer

Section titled “Look closer”

Field Inspect

Section titled “Field Inspect”

Field Inspect takes one named element on its own, with its ours, base and theirs versions, and runs the driver on just that element in a new tab. It helps when an element is deep inside a large conflict, or when you want to check a move.

  1. Right-click a line and choose Inspect element. The Workbench picks the innermost element on that line that has an id or a name. Clicking a moved by note in a pane does the same for the element that moved.
  2. A tab named Inspect: with the element’s name opens. Resolve it there like any case.
  3. Press Apply to parent at the right end of the ribbon. The tab’s result replaces that element in the original case’s merged result. If the element sat inside a conflict, it replaces the whole conflict.

An Inspect tab has no file of its own, so it has no save. Field Inspect is not offered in a text merge.

Unresolved fallbacks

Section titled “Unresolved fallbacks”

Most elements in a manuscript have an id or name, and the driver pairs ours, base and theirs by it. Elements without one, like the <v> inside a field, are paired by how alike they look and then by position. A pairing by position can be wrong, so when any member of a group was paired that way, the driver counts the group as an unresolved fallback. The count asks for a glance. It never blocks saving.

In the Structural diff, each group shows as a row like v ×1 with a chip such as 1 member · 0 to review, where “to review” counts members still in conflict. Open the row to act on the group:

  • approve, pairing verified marks the group as checked.
  • Replace the whole run: all ours, union or all theirs replaces every member in the merged result with one side’s run, or with both. The Workbench asks you to confirm first.
  • take ours and take theirs on a member settle that member when it is in conflict.
  • sub-merge lists how each member was paired and how confident the pairing is.

Files that need care

Section titled “Files that need care”

When a file will not parse

Section titled “When a file will not parse”

A manuscript that is not well-formed XML will not load in Duck Creek. The Workbench marks the spot three ways:

  • A red squiggle in each pane that has the problem. Hover it for the parser’s reason.
  • not well-formed in the merged result’s header. Click it to jump to the spot.
  • In the ribbon note, the line and column are links to the spot in that pane.

Fix the text in the merged pane. The marks update as you type. Save & finish will not write the file until the merged result parses. HTML entities such as &nbsp; do not count as problems, since Duck Creek loads them.

Text merges

Section titled “Text merges”

The driver’s XML merge is for Duck Creek manuscripts only. Any other file, or a manuscript the XML engine cannot read, gets git’s normal line merge instead, and the Workbench opens it as a text merge. The ribbon always says why, for example Text merge: the root is <settings>, not <ManuScript>, so git’s line merge ran.

In a text merge:

  • Conflicts are blocks of lines. Step to them and take sides as usual, or edit.
  • The Structural diff reads No structure in a text merge.
  • Field Inspect, Reformat and defect reports are not available.

Troubleshooting lists each reason and what to do about it.
